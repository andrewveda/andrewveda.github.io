About Me

I am an English academic with interdisciplinary interests spanning literature, language pedagogy, environmental humanities, and aesthetics. I teach English not as a static subject, but as a living system of choices.

My work centres on pattern-recognition — the same cognitive skill that connects grammar, chess, poetry, and problem-solving. I believe learning is not absorption, but self-creation.

Research & Intellectual Work

My research engages with environmental humanities, especially zoosemiotics and phytosemiotics, arguing that nature itself participates in meaning-making.

I work extensively on classical Tamil literature, particularly Kuṟuntokai, foregrounding female gaze, emotional agency, and narrative dominance. I have translated the entire text into acrostic verse.

I am also developing an aesthetic theory that equates aesthetics with individuality and growth rather than surface beauty.

Teaching Philosophy

I design gamified, ICT-based learning experiences for engineering students, blending clarity, curiosity, creativity, and agency.

My classrooms treat communication as performance, grammar as intuition, and assessment as play — especially for ESL students.
